𝗧𝘄𝗼 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗺 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗹𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝗻 𝗮 𝘆𝗲𝗮𝗿 𝗼𝗹𝗱.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝗶𝗴 𝗳𝘂𝗻𝗱𝘀 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗺𝗶𝗱𝗱𝗹𝗲 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗮𝗰𝗸.

𝗠𝗼𝘀𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗺𝗼𝗻𝗲𝘆 𝘀𝗶𝘁𝘀 𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗼𝘀𝗲 𝗯𝗶𝗴 𝗳𝘂𝗻𝗱𝘀.
The ten largest hold 73.98% of the ₹4,65,669 Cr in small-cap funds. Their median return this year is 12.54%, compared with 18.08% for the other 26.

None of this means smaller funds will keep leading. Nine months is a short period, small-cap leadership changes quickly, and the three funds launched this year (Groww, Abakkus and The Wealth Company) are measured from their first NAV rather than from 1 January, which is a shorter window.

Still, it's a useful reminder that in 2026, a bigger small-cap fund did not mean a better-performing one.

Do you look at fund size when you choose a small-cap fund, or only at the track record?

## Caveats

- **Mixed windows (disclosed in the post and on slide 2):** 33 funds use 31 Dec 2025 → 25 Sep 2026. The 3 funds launched in 2026 use first NAV → 25 Sep 2026: Groww 2 Feb, Abakkus 20 Mar, The Wealth Company 30 Mar (+20.04%, #11). Abakkus and The Wealth Company started after the March 2026 fall, so their windows began at a lower market level.
- **Median of the other 26** includes the three since-launch funds. For the 10 largest vs the other 23 full-year funds only, the figures are +12.54% vs +17.72% (`facts_insights.json`).
- **Samco Small Cap** launched 14 Nov 2025, so it has a 31 Dec 2025 NAV and is on the full-YTD basis.
- **Bandhan Small Cap** AUM grew steadily from ₹9,234 Cr (Jan 2025) to ₹34,162 Cr (Aug 2026). Its holdings sum to 87–92% of AUM every month (a disclosure structure, not an error).
- **Growth-plan filter fix:** Samco was previously dropped because its plan name has no "Growth". Fixed in `mf_facts.py`.
